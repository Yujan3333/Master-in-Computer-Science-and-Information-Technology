# 1. Introduction

**Photographic imaging** is the process of capturing images of the Earth's surface using **cameras mounted on aircraft, drones, or satellites**.

Unlike digital imaging, photographic imaging traditionally uses **photographic film** to record images.

It is mainly used for:

* Topographic mapping
* Land-use surveys
* Urban planning
* Agriculture
* Forestry
* Disaster assessment

---

# 2. Camera Systems

A **camera system** is the device used to capture aerial photographs.

## Main Components

```text
Earth Surface
      │
 Reflected Light
      │
      ▼
    Lens
      │
      ▼
 Aperture
      │
      ▼
 Shutter
      │
      ▼
 Film / Digital Sensor
      │
      ▼
 Photograph
```

### Components

### A. Lens

* Collects reflected light.
* Focuses the light onto the film or sensor.
* Determines image sharpness.

---

### B. Aperture

* Opening through which light enters.
* Controls the amount of light reaching the film.

Large aperture → More light

Small aperture → Less light

---

### C. Shutter

* Controls the exposure time.
* Opens and closes quickly while taking a photograph.

Fast shutter:

* Less blur

Slow shutter:

* More light
* More chance of motion blur

---

### D. Film / Digital Sensor

Records the image formed by the lens.

Older systems:

* Photographic film

Modern systems:

* CCD or CMOS digital sensors

---

# 3. Types of Camera

---

## A. Frame Camera

* Captures the entire image in one exposure.
* Most common camera used in aerial photography.

### Advantages

* High geometric accuracy
* High image quality
* Suitable for photogrammetry

### Disadvantages

* Covers a smaller area per image.

### Applications

* Mapping
* Surveying
* Engineering

---

## B. Panoramic Camera

* Rotates while exposing the film.
* Produces a wide panoramic image.

### Advantages

* Large ground coverage

### Disadvantages

* Image distortion near edges
* Complex geometry

### Applications

* Large-area reconnaissance
* Military surveys

---

## C. Strip Camera

* Continuously records a narrow strip while the aircraft moves.

### Advantages

* Continuous coverage

### Disadvantages

* Lower geometric accuracy

### Applications

* Highways
* Railways
* Rivers
* Pipelines

---

## Comparison of Camera Types

| Feature          | Frame  | Panoramic | Strip      |
| ---------------- | ------ | --------- | ---------- |
| Exposure         | Single | Rotating  | Continuous |
| Coverage         | Small  | Large     | Long Strip |
| Distortion       | Low    | High      | Medium     |
| Mapping Accuracy | High   | Medium    | Low        |

---

# 4. Filters

## Definition

A **filter** is a transparent material placed in front of the camera lens to allow only selected wavelengths of light to pass.

It improves image quality and enhances specific features.

---

## Functions of Filters

* Reduce haze
* Improve contrast
* Increase image clarity
* Highlight vegetation
* Protect the camera lens

---

## Types of Filters

### Blue Filter

* Passes blue light.
* Used in water and atmospheric studies.

---

### Green Filter

* Enhances vegetation.
* Improves plant visibility.

---

### Yellow Filter

* Reduces atmospheric haze.
* Improves contrast.

---

### Red Filter

* Improves contrast between vegetation and soil.
* Useful for geological mapping.

---

### Infrared Filter

* Passes near-infrared radiation.
* Healthy vegetation appears brighter.

---

## Comparison of Filters

| Filter   | Main Use           |
| -------- | ------------------ |
| Blue     | Water studies      |
| Green    | Vegetation         |
| Yellow   | Haze reduction     |
| Red      | Geological mapping |
| Infrared | Crop health        |

---

# 5. Film

Photographic film is a light-sensitive material used to record aerial photographs.

---

## A. Black & White (Panchromatic) Film

Characteristics

* Records gray tones.
* High spatial resolution.
* Cheapest film.

Applications

* Mapping
* Surveying

---

## B. Natural Color Film

Records

* Red
* Green
* Blue

Produces images similar to human vision.

Applications

* General photography
* Land-use mapping

---

## C. Color Infrared (False Color) Film

Sensitive to:

* Green
* Red
* Near Infrared

Healthy vegetation appears **red**.

Applications

* Agriculture
* Forestry
* Crop monitoring

---

## D. Infrared Film

Sensitive to infrared radiation.

Applications

* Forest fire detection
* Water studies
* Environmental monitoring

---

## Film Comparison

| Film Type     | Vegetation Color   | Main Application    |
| ------------- | ------------------ | ------------------- |
| Black & White | Gray               | Mapping             |
| Natural Color | Green              | General Photography |
| False Color   | Red                | Agriculture         |
| Infrared      | Bright IR response | Environment         |

# 6. Geometry of Aerial Photography

Geometry explains how the camera is positioned relative to the Earth's surface.

---

## A. Vertical Photograph

The camera axis points **straight downward (toward the nadir).**

### Characteristics

* Uniform scale
* Minimal distortion
* Best for mapping
* Accurate measurements

### Applications

* Topographic maps
* GIS
* Photogrammetry

---

## B. Oblique Photograph

The camera axis is tilted.

### Types

#### Low Oblique

* Horizon not visible.

#### High Oblique

* Horizon visible.

### Characteristics

* Covers larger area.
* Variable scale.
* More distortion.

### Applications

* Landscape studies
* Military reconnaissance
* Tourism

---

## Vertical vs Oblique Photograph

| Vertical           | Oblique                      |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| Camera points down | Camera tilted                |
| Uniform scale      | Variable scale               |
| Less distortion    | More distortion              |
| Best for mapping   | Better visual interpretation |

---

# 7. Scale of Aerial Photograph

The **scale** is the ratio between a distance on the photograph and the corresponding distance on the ground.

### Formula

$$
\text{Scale}=\frac{\text{Distance on Photo}}{\text{Distance on Ground}}
$$

### Example

If:

* 1 cm on the photo = 500 m on the ground

Convert to the same units:

* 500 m = 50,000 cm

Therefore,

$$
\text{Scale}=\frac{1}{50,000}
$$

or

**1 : 50,000**

---

## Types of Scale

### Large Scale

Example:

1 : 5,000

* High detail
* Small area covered

---

### Medium Scale

Example:

1 : 25,000

* Moderate detail

---

### Small Scale

Example:

1 : 100,000

* Covers a large area
* Less detail

# 8. Ideal Time and Atmosphere for Aerial Remote Sensing

For obtaining high-quality aerial photographs, the following conditions are preferred.

---

## Ideal Time

* Around noon (10 AM–2 PM)
* Maximum sunlight
* Minimum shadow
* Stable weather

---

## Ideal Atmospheric Conditions

* Clear sky
* No clouds
* Low humidity
* No rain
* Low dust
* Low haze
* Good visibility

---

## Why are these conditions ideal?

They reduce:

* Atmospheric scattering
* Absorption
* Shadows

leading to clearer and more accurate photographs.

# Advantages of Photographic Imaging

* High spatial resolution
* Easy visual interpretation
* Permanent photographic record
* Cost-effective for local surveys
* Suitable for photogrammetry

---

# Limitations of Photographic Imaging

* Depends on sunlight
* Affected by cloud cover
* Limited spectral information
* Film can degrade over time
* Smaller coverage compared to satellites
