## Uncertain Knowledge 

### Definition

**Uncertain knowledge** refers to situations where we **cannot predict the exact outcome** because information is incomplete, noisy, or unpredictable. Therefore, decisions cannot rely only on classical true/false logic.

### Airport Example

Suppose:

* $A_t$ = **Leave for the airport $t$ minutes before the flight.**

Question:

> **Will $A_t$ get me to the airport on time?**

The answer is uncertain because many factors are unknown.

### Causes of Uncertainty

1. **Partial observability**

   * We do not know everything.
   * Example: Road conditions, traffic, other drivers' plans.

2. **Noisy sensors**

   * Information may be inaccurate.
   * Example: Radio or traffic reports may be wrong.

3. **Uncertainty in action outcomes**

   * Unexpected events may occur.
   * Example: Flat tire, vehicle breakdown, accident.

4. **Complexity of prediction**

   * Traffic and road conditions are difficult to model and predict accurately.

### Why Pure Logic Fails

A purely logical approach has three problems:

1. **May produce false conclusions**

   * Example: "Leaving 25 minutes early will definitely get me there on time."

2. **May become too restrictive**

   * Example:

     > "Leaving 25 minutes early works **only if** there is no accident, no rain, no flat tire, etc."
   * Such conditions are impractical for decision making.

3. **May give impractical solutions**

   * Example:

     > Leaving **1440 minutes (1 day)** before the flight almost guarantees arriving on time, but it means staying overnight at the airport.

### Conclusion

Since real-world situations contain uncertainty, **classical logic alone is insufficient**. We need **probabilistic reasoning** to estimate the likelihood of different outcomes and make practical decisions under uncertainty.

## 5-Mark Exam Answer

**Uncertain knowledge** refers to situations where complete and accurate information is unavailable, making it impossible to predict outcomes with certainty. The airport example illustrates this problem, where the action $A_t$ (leaving $t$ minutes before a flight) may or may not result in arriving on time because of factors such as **partial observability**, **noisy sensors**, **uncertain action outcomes**, and the **complexity of predicting traffic**. A purely logical approach may produce false conclusions, require too many conditions to be practical, or suggest unrealistic solutions. Therefore, uncertain knowledge is better handled using **probabilistic reasoning**, which makes decisions based on the likelihood of different outcomes.
