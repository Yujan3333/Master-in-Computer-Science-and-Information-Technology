

| **Aspect**                     | **Booch OOD**                                                                 | **Traditional OOD Methods**                                                |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Specificity**             | A complete, named methodology with defined steps and notations.               | Refers to general object-oriented design practices, not always methodical. |
| **2. Diagram Types**           | Uses 6 specific diagrams: Class, Object, State, Module, Process, Interaction. | May use fewer or less standardized diagrams; varies by method.             |
| **3. Process Structure**       | Has both Macro (lifecycle) and Micro (daily dev) processes.                   | Process structure is often informal or loosely defined.                    |
| **4. Architecture Focus**      | Strong focus on architectural modeling (e.g., modules, subsystems).           | Architecture is important but may not be deeply modeled or formalized.     |
| **5. Iterative Nature**        | Explicitly iterative with built-in feedback/refinement loops.                 | May be iterative in practice, but not always methodically enforced.        |
| **6. Notation**                | Own formal notation (precursor to UML).                                       | Often informal or varies (before UML became standard).                     |
| **7. Use Case Role**           | Use cases exist but not the central starting point.                           | Some traditional methods (like OOSE) are use-case driven.                  |
| **8. System Size Suitability** | Best suited for large, complex systems.                                       | Often simpler, more adaptable for small to mid-sized projects.             |
| **9. Concurrency Support**     | Supports modeling of concurrent systems (e.g., via process diagrams).         | Concurrency may not be explicitly addressed in all traditional methods.    |
| **10. Historical Impact**      | Influenced UML significantly (alongside OMT and Objectory).                   | Contributed indirectly to UML through generalized OOD practices.           |
