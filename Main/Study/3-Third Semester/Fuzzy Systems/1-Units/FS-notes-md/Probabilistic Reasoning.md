## Probabilistic Reasoning 

### Definition

**Probabilistic reasoning** is a method of making decisions under **uncertainty** by assigning probabilities to events or hypotheses. It is also called **Bayesian reasoning** because it is based on **Bayes' theorem**, which updates the probability of a hypothesis when new evidence is available.

### Key Points

* Used when information is **uncertain or incomplete**.
* Combines:

  * **Probability theory** → Handles uncertainty.
  * **Deductive logic** → Provides logical structure.
* Extends traditional true/false logic by using **probabilities** instead of only binary truth values.
* **Bayes' rule** analyzes past uncertain situations to determine the probability that a particular cause produced an observed outcome.
* The updated probability is then used to **predict future outcomes**.

### Applications

* Medical diagnosis (diagnostic accuracy of tests)
* Artificial Intelligence
* Expert systems
* Decision support systems
* Prediction under uncertainty

### 5-Mark Exam Answer

**Probabilistic reasoning** is a technique used to make decisions under uncertainty by assigning probabilities to different events or hypotheses. It is also known as **Bayesian reasoning** because it is based on **Bayes' theorem**, which updates the probability of a hypothesis using new evidence. It combines the uncertainty-handling ability of **probability theory** with the structured reasoning of **deductive logic**. Unlike classical logic, which gives only true or false results, probabilistic reasoning expresses conclusions using probabilities. It is widely used in medical diagnosis, artificial intelligence, expert systems, and decision-making under uncertainty.
