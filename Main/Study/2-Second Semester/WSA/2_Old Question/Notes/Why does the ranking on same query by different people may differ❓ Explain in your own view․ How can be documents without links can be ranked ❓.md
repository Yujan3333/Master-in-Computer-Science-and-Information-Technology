### **1️⃣ Why ranking for the same query may differ for different people**

In my view, rankings differ because **search results are personalized**. Even when the query is the same, search systems consider **user-specific and context-specific factors**, such as:

* **User location** (local results differ by place)
* **Search history & preferences**
* **Language settings**
* **Device used (mobile/desktop)**
* **Time and trending topics**

👉 As a result, the search engine tries to show results that are **most relevant to each individual user**, causing different rankings for the same query.

---

### **2️⃣ How documents without links can be ranked**

Documents without links can still be ranked using **content-based and usage-based factors**, such as:

* **Keyword relevance** (TF-IDF, BM25)
* **Content quality and freshness**
* **User behavior** (clicks, dwell time)
* **Metadata** (title, headings, tags)
* **Semantic similarity** (meaning of text)

👉 Even without hyperlinks, a document can rank well if its **content is relevant and useful**.

---

### **Short exam-ready answer**

> Rankings differ for the same query due to personalization factors like location and user history. Documents without links can be ranked using content relevance, metadata, and user behavior analysis.
