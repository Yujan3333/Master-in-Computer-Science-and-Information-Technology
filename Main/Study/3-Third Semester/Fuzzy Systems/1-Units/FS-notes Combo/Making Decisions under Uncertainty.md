# Making Decisions Under Uncertainty 

## Definition

**Making decisions under uncertainty** is the process of choosing the **best action** when the outcome is uncertain. The decision depends on:

1. The **probability** of each possible outcome.
2. The **utility (preference or value)** of that outcome.

The notes explain that **Decision Theory = Probability Theory + Utility Theory**. 

---

# Airport Example

### Problem

You want to **reach the airport on time**.

Suppose you estimate the following probabilities:

| Action                                | Probability of Reaching on Time |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Leave 25 minutes early ($A_{25}$)     | 0.04 (4%)                       |
| Leave 90 minutes early ($A_{90}$)     | 0.70 (70%)                      |
| Leave 120 minutes early ($A_{120}$)   | 0.95 (95%)                      |
| Leave 1440 minutes early ($A_{1440}$) | 0.9999 (99.99%)                 |

---

# Which Action Should You Choose?

The answer is **not simply the action with the highest probability**.

It depends on your **preferences (utility)**.

For example:

* If **missing the flight is very costly**, you may choose **$A_{120}$** or even **$A_{1440}$**.
* If **waiting at the airport for a long time is inconvenient**, you may choose **$A_{90}$**.
* Choosing **$A_{25}$** is risky because it has only a **4% chance** of arriving on time.

Thus, the best decision depends on balancing:

* **Probability of success**, and
* **Personal preferences (utility).**

---

# Utility Theory

**Utility Theory** is used to represent a person's **preferences or satisfaction** for different outcomes.

It answers questions such as:

* How bad is missing the flight?
* How inconvenient is waiting several hours at the airport?
* Which outcome provides the greatest overall benefit?

---

# Decision Theory

The notes define:

> **Decision Theory = Utility Theory + Probability Theory**

This means:

* **Probability Theory** tells us **how likely** each outcome is.
* **Utility Theory** tells us **how valuable or desirable** each outcome is.
* **Decision Theory** combines both to choose the **best action**.

---

# Rational Decision

According to the notes, a **rational decision** depends on:

1. The **importance of different goals**.
2. The **likelihood** of achieving those goals.
3. The **degree to which** the goals are achieved. 

---

# Key Points

* Decisions are made under **uncertainty**.
* Probability measures the **likelihood** of success.
* Utility measures the **value or preference** of an outcome.
* **Decision Theory** combines probability and utility.
* A rational decision considers **both probability and utility**, not probability alone.

---

# Example

Suppose:

* Missing the flight is **very expensive**.
* Waiting at the airport is only **slightly inconvenient**.

Even though $A_{1440}$ means waiting a long time, it may still be the better choice because the cost of missing the flight is much greater.

---

# 5-Mark Exam Answer

**Making decisions under uncertainty** is the process of selecting the best action when outcomes are uncertain. The decision is based on both the **probability of success** and the **utility (preference or value)** of each outcome. In the airport example, different departure times have different probabilities of arriving on time. The best choice depends not only on these probabilities but also on personal preferences, such as the importance of avoiding a missed flight versus the inconvenience of waiting at the airport. **Utility Theory** represents these preferences, while **Probability Theory** measures the likelihood of outcomes. Together, they form **Decision Theory**, which helps make rational decisions under uncertainty.
