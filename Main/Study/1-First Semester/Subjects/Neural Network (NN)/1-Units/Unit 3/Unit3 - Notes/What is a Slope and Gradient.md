### 📏 What is a **Slope**?
- [Slope](../../../../../../2-Second%20Semester/Machine%20Learning/1-Units/Unit2/Unit%202%20-%20Notes/Slope.md)

Imagine a **straight line** on a graph.

- The **slope** tells you how steep the line is.
    
- It's the **rise over run** — how much the line goes **up or down** (rise) for how much it goes **left or right** (run).

If a line goes **up 2 units** for every **1 unit to the right**, the slope is:

### 🧮 What is a **Gradient**?
The **gradient** is just a **fancy word** for slope, but used for functions with **more than one input** (like a hill that goes up and down in many directions).

- It tells you the direction and steepness of the **steepest slope**.
    
- In math, it's made of **partial derivatives** (one for each input).

---
---
### Summary:
- **Slope**: Steepness of a line.
    
- **Gradient**: Steepness and direction of a hill or surface.
    
- Both help us understand **how to move to get lower** (which is how machines learn — by reducing error).
