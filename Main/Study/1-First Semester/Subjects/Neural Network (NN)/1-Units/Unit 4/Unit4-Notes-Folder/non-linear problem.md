A **non-linear problem** is a type of mathematical problem where the relationship between variables is **not linear** — meaning the output is not directly proportional to the input.

In simpler terms:

- In **linear problems**, equations form straight lines (like `y = 2x + 3`).
    
- In **non-linear problems**, equations can include powers, products of variables, or other complex functions (like `y = x² + 3x` or `y = sin(x)`), and they do **not form straight lines** when graphed.
    

---

### Characteristics of Non-Linear Problems:

1. **Equations include**:
    
    - Powers or roots (e.g. `x²`, `√x`)
        
    - Products of variables (e.g. `xy`)
        
    - Trigonometric functions (`sin`, `cos`, etc.)
        
    - Exponential or logarithmic terms (`e^x`, `log(x)`)
        
2. **Graph Shape**:
    
    - Curved lines, circles, parabolas, or other complex shapes.
        
3. **Behavior**:
    
    - Output changes in a non-proportional way as inputs change.
        
    - Small changes in input can cause large changes in output (and vice versa).
        

---

### Examples:

|Type|Equation|Notes|
|---|---|---|
|Linear|`y = 3x + 5`|Straight line|
|Non-linear|`y = x² + 2`|Parabola (curved graph)|
|Non-linear|`y = sin(x)`|Wave-like graph|
|Non-linear|`xy = 1`|Hyperbola|

---

### Real-Life Examples:

- **Physics**: Projectile motion (parabolic path)
    
- **Economics**: Supply and demand curves
    
- **Machine Learning**: Non-linear classifiers (like neural networks)
    
- **Optimization**: When constraints or the objective function are non-linear
