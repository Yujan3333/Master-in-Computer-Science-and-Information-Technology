# Elements of Visual Perception

Visual perception explains how humans interpret images. In digital image processing, three key elements are:

1. Human Eye
2. Image Formation in the Eye
3. Brightness Adaptation and Discrimination

---

# 1. Human Eye

The human eye is the primary sensing organ for visual information.

## Structure of the Eye

* **Cornea** → Outer transparent layer; helps focus light
* **Iris** → Controls amount of light entering (like aperture)
* **Pupil** → Opening through which light enters
* **Lens** → Focuses light onto retina
* **Retina** → Contains light-sensitive cells
* **Optic nerve** → Sends signals to brain

---

## Photoreceptors in Retina

### 1. Rods

* Sensitive to low light (night vision)
* Do not detect color
* High sensitivity, low resolution

### 2. Cones

* Responsible for color vision
* Work in bright light
* High resolution, low sensitivity

👉 Cones are concentrated in the **fovea** (center of vision).

---

# 2. Image Formation in the Eye

The eye works like a camera.

## Process

1. Light from an object enters through the **cornea**
2. Passes through **pupil** (controlled by iris)
3. **Lens** focuses the light
4. An **inverted image** is formed on the retina
5. Photoreceptors convert light into electrical signals
6. Signals are sent to brain → interpreted as image

---

## Important Points

* Image formed on retina is **real and inverted**
* Brain processes and interprets it correctly
* Focusing ability is called **accommodation**

---

# 3. Brightness Adaptation and Discrimination

Human vision can handle a very wide range of light intensities.

---

## (a) Brightness Adaptation

* Human eye can adapt to light levels from **very dark to very bright**
* Range ≈ $10^{10}$ intensity levels
* But at any moment, only a limited range is visible

👉 This is called **adaptation level**

---

## (b) Brightness Discrimination

* Ability to distinguish small changes in intensity

### Weber’s Law:

$$\frac{\Delta I}{I}=constant$$

Where:

* $I$ = background intensity
* $\Delta I$ = smallest detectable change

---

## Interpretation

* At **low intensity** → small changes are noticeable
* At **high intensity** → larger changes needed

---

## Key Concept: Just Noticeable Difference (JND)

* Minimum intensity change the eye can detect
* Depends on background brightness

---

# Summary

| Element                   | Description                                    |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Human Eye                 | Senses light using rods and cones              |
| Image Formation           | Light focused on retina forming inverted image |
| Brightness Adaptation     | Eye adjusts to different light levels          |
| Brightness Discrimination | Detects intensity differences (Weber’s Law)    |

---

# Key Exam Points

* Rods → night vision, Cones → color vision
* Image formed is **inverted on retina**
* Brightness range is large but perception is limited at a time
* Weber’s law is important for intensity discrimination
