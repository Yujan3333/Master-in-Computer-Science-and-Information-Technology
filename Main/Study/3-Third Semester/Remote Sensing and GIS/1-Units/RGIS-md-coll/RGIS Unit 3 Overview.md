# Unit 3: Photographic Imaging (Overview)

**Exam Weight:** 3 Hours (Theory)

This chapter explains how photographs are used in remote sensing, especially **aerial photography**. It covers cameras, filters, films, image geometry, and the ideal conditions for taking aerial photographs.

---

# 1. Introduction

**Photographic imaging** is the process of capturing images of the Earth's surface using **cameras mounted on aircraft, drones, or satellites**.

Before digital sensors became common, aerial photographs were the primary source of remote sensing data.

Even today, aerial photography is widely used for:

* Mapping
* Urban planning
* Agriculture
* Disaster assessment
* Engineering surveys

---

# 2. Camera Systems

A **camera system** records light reflected from the Earth's surface onto a film or digital sensor.

### Main Components

* Lens
* Shutter
* Aperture
* Film or Digital Sensor
* Camera Body

### Working Principle

1. Sunlight illuminates the Earth's surface.
2. Objects reflect light.
3. The camera lens focuses the reflected light.
4. The film or sensor records the image.

A **filter** is a transparent material placed in front of the camera lens to **allow certain wavelengths to pass and block others**.

### Purpose

* Improve image quality
* Reduce haze
* Increase contrast
* Highlight specific features

### Common Types

* Blue Filter
* Green Filter
* Red Filter
* Yellow Filter
* Infrared Filter

### Example

An **infrared filter** helps distinguish healthy vegetation because plants strongly reflect infrared radiation.

# 5. Film

Before digital sensors, cameras recorded images on **photographic film**.

### Types of Film

## A. Black and White (Panchromatic)

* Records shades of gray.
* High spatial resolution.

---

## B. True Color Film

* Records Red, Green, and Blue.
* Similar to human vision.

---

## C. Color Infrared (False Color)

* Sensitive to near-infrared.
* Healthy vegetation appears **red**.

---

## D. Infrared Film

* Detects infrared radiation.
* Used for vegetation and water studies.

---

## Comparison of Film Types

| Film           | Appearance             | Main Use               |
| -------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Black & White  | Gray                   | Mapping                |
| True Color     | Natural colors         | General photography    |
| Color Infrared | Vegetation appears red | Agriculture & forestry |
| Infrared       | Infrared response      | Environmental studies  |

# 6. Geometry of Aerial Photography

This topic explains how the camera is positioned relative to the Earth's surface.

## A. Vertical Photograph

The camera points **straight downward (toward the nadir).**

### Advantages

* Minimum distortion
* Uniform scale
* Best for mapping
* Accurate measurements

---

## B. Oblique Photograph

The camera is tilted.

Types:

### Low Oblique

* Horizon is **not visible**.

### High Oblique

* Horizon **is visible**.

### Advantages

* Larger area covered
* Better visual interpretation

### Disadvantages

* More distortion
* Scale varies across the image

---

## Vertical vs Oblique Photography

| Vertical                    | Oblique                      |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Camera points straight down | Camera is tilted             |
| Uniform scale               | Variable scale               |
| Less distortion             | More distortion              |
| Accurate mapping            | Better visual interpretation |

---

# 7. Scale of Aerial Photograph

The **scale** represents the relationship between a distance on the photograph and the corresponding distance on the ground.

### Formula

$$
\text{Scale}=\frac{\text{Distance on Photograph}}{\text{Distance on Ground}}
$$

### Example

If:

* 1 cm on the photograph = 1000 cm on the ground

Then

$$
Scale=\frac{1}{1000}
$$

Written as

**1 : 1000**

### Interpretation

* Large-scale photo (e.g., 1:5,000): More detail, smaller area.
* Small-scale photo (e.g., 1:100,000): Less detail, larger area.

# 8. Ideal Time and Atmosphere for Aerial Remote Sensing

For high-quality aerial photographs:

### Ideal Time

* Around noon
* Maximum sunlight
* Minimum shadow

---

### Ideal Weather

* Clear sky
* No clouds
* No rain
* Low humidity
* Low dust
* Good visibility

---

### Why?

These conditions reduce atmospheric effects such as scattering and absorption, resulting in clearer images.

# Advantages of Photographic Imaging

* High spatial resolution
* Easy visual interpretation
* Permanent record
* Cost-effective for small areas
* Useful for mapping and planning

---

# Limitations of Photographic Imaging

* Affected by weather and clouds
* Depends on sunlight (for optical photography)
* Limited spectral information compared to multispectral sensors
* Film can deteriorate over time
* Covers smaller areas than satellites

# Quick Revision (1-Minute Summary)

* **Photographic Imaging:** Capturing Earth's surface using cameras.
* **Frame Camera:** Captures the whole image at once; most common for mapping.
* **Panoramic Camera:** Rotates to capture a wide area.
* **Strip Camera:** Produces continuous strip images.
* **Filters:** Improve image quality by allowing selected wavelengths to pass.
* **Film Types:** Black & White, True Color, Color Infrared, Infrared.
* **Vertical Photograph:** Camera points straight down; best for mapping.
* **Oblique Photograph:** Camera is tilted; covers a larger area but has more distortion.
* **Scale Formula:** $\text{Scale}=\dfrac{\text{Photo Distance}}{\text{Ground Distance}}$
* **Ideal Conditions:** Clear sky, low humidity, good visibility, and midday sunlight.
