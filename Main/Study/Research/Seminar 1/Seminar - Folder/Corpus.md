
### **What is a Corpus?**
   
   A **corpus** (plural: corpora) is a **collection of authentic text or audio data**, typically organized into structured datasets. “Authentic” means the content is created by native speakers or writers of the language or dialect. A corpus can include diverse sources such as:
   
   * Newspapers, novels, blogs, recipes
   * Tweets, emails, and social media posts
   * Radio broadcasts, TV shows, and movies
   * Transcribed interviews and conversations
   
   In **Natural Language Processing (NLP)** and **Machine Learning (ML)**, a corpus provides the raw material—text or speech—that algorithms learn from. The goal is to build systems that understand, interpret, or generate human language based on patterns in this data.
   
   ---
   
### **Features of a Good Corpus**
   
   1. **Large Corpus Size**
   
      * More data improves model learning and performance, especially for complex tasks like sentiment analysis or machine translation.
   
   2. **High-Quality Data**
   
      * The data should be accurate, well-formed, and representative of real usage. Errors in the data can mislead models during training.
   
   3. **Clean Data**
   
      * Remove noise like duplicates, formatting errors, or irrelevant information. Clean datasets are easier to process and yield better results.
   
   4. **Balanced Content**
   
      * A good corpus reflects different aspects of language use (e.g., formal vs informal, various topics and styles). This prevents bias and improves generalization.
   
   ---
   
### **Challenges in Creating a Corpus**
   
   5. **Defining the Right Data**
   
      * Knowing what type of language data is needed to solve your specific NLP task is the first step.
   
   6. **Data Availability**
   
      * Publicly available data in many languages—especially low-resource ones like Nepali—is limited.
   
   7. **Data Quality**
   
      * Collecting authentic, high-quality, and error-free data is often time-consuming and resource-intensive.
   
   8. **Data Adequacy**
   
      * Some domains (like medical or legal language) may not have enough accessible examples for effective training.
