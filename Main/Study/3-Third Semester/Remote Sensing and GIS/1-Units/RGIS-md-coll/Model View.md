## C. Model View of GIS

The **Model View** considers GIS as a **decision-support and analytical tool**. It focuses on using geographic data to **analyze patterns, solve problems, predict future situations, and support decision-making**, rather than just storing or displaying maps.

### Main Focus

### 1. Decision-Making

GIS helps people make better decisions by analyzing geographic information.

**Examples:**

* Choosing the best location for a new hospital or school.
* Selecting the safest evacuation route during a flood.
* Deciding where to build a new road.

**Example:**
A city government uses GIS to determine the best location for a fire station so that emergency response times are minimized.

---

### 2. Prediction

GIS can **predict future events or conditions** by analyzing existing spatial data and trends.

**Examples:**

* Predicting flood-prone areas.
* Forecasting urban expansion.
* Estimating areas at risk of landslides.
* Predicting the spread of forest fires.

**Example:**
By combining rainfall, elevation, and river data, GIS predicts which villages are likely to be flooded.

---

### 3. Spatial Analysis

**Definition:**

**Spatial analysis** is the process of examining the **location, distance, direction, relationships, and patterns** of geographic features to answer location-based questions.

In simple words:

> **Spatial analysis = analyzing "where" things are and how they are related in space.**

Unlike normal data analysis (which answers **what**), spatial analysis answers **where**, **how far**, **which is nearest**, **what overlaps**, and **what lies inside an area**.

### Examples of Spatial Analysis

#### a) Buffer Analysis

Creates a zone around a feature.

Example:

* Find all schools within **500 m** of a hospital.
* Find houses within **1 km** of a river.

---

#### b) Overlay Analysis

Combines multiple map layers.

Example:

* Overlay rainfall + soil + slope maps to identify suitable agricultural land.

---

#### c) Network Analysis

Finds the best route through a road network.

Example:

* Shortest route for an ambulance.
* Fastest delivery path.

---

#### d) Proximity Analysis

Finds the nearest feature.

Example:

* Which hospital is closest to an accident location?

---

#### e) Terrain Analysis

Studies elevation and landforms.

Example:

* Identify steep slopes for landslide risk.
* Determine water flow direction.

---

## Exam Answer (5 Marks)

**Model View of GIS:**
The Model View considers GIS as a tool for **analysis, modeling, prediction, and decision-making**. It uses geographic data to solve real-world problems rather than only storing maps.

Its main functions are:

* **Decision-making:** Helps select the best location or plan based on spatial data.
* **Prediction:** Forecasts future events such as floods, urban growth, or landslides.
* **Spatial analysis:** Examines the location, distance, direction, and relationships between geographic features using techniques like **buffer, overlay, network, and proximity analysis**.

This view enables planners, engineers, and governments to make informed decisions based on geographic information.
