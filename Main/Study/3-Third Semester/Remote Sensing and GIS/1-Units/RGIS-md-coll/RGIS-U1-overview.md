# Unit 1: Concept of Remote Sensing (Overview)

# 1. Introduction

Remote sensing is the **science and art** of obtaining information about objects, areas, or phenomena **without physically touching them**.

Instead of going to the location, we use:

* Satellites
* Aircraft
* Drones
* Sensors

to collect information.

### Example

Instead of visiting Mount Everest to measure snow cover, satellites capture images and scientists analyze them.

---

# 2. Distance and Definition of Remote Sensing

### Definition

> **Remote sensing is the process of acquiring information about an object or area from a distance without direct physical contact by detecting and measuring reflected or emitted electromagnetic energy.**

Key words:

* Remote = From a distance
* Sensing = Detecting information

---

# 3. Remote Sensing: Art and/or Science

This is a common theory question.

Remote sensing is **both an art and a science.**

## Science

It involves scientific principles such as:

* Physics
* Electromagnetic radiation
* Sensor technology
* Computer processing
* Mathematics

## Art

Image interpretation often depends on human experience and judgment.

Two analysts may interpret an image differently depending on their expertise.

**Conclusion**

> Remote sensing combines scientific measurement with human interpretation, making it both a science and an art.

---

# 4. Data

Remote sensing collects **data** about Earth's surface.

The data may include:

* Images
* Temperature
* Vegetation
* Soil moisture
* Water bodies
* Land use
* Elevation

### Types

* Analog data
* Digital data

Most modern systems use digital data.

---

# 5. Remote Sensing Process

This is the **most important topic** in Unit 1.

The process follows a sequence.

```
Energy Source
      ↓
Atmosphere
      ↓
Target/Object
      ↓
Sensor
      ↓
Transmission
      ↓
Processing
      ↓
Interpretation
      ↓
Application
```

Let's study each step.

---

# Step 1: Source of Energy

Every remote sensing system requires energy.

Usually the source is

☀ Sun

Some systems generate their own energy.

Examples:

* Radar
* LiDAR

These are called **active remote sensing** systems.

---

# Step 2: Interaction with Atmosphere

Energy travels through the atmosphere before reaching Earth.

During travel it experiences

* Absorption
* Scattering
* Transmission

These affect image quality.

---

# Step 3: Interaction with Target

The energy reaches an object such as

* Forest
* Building
* Water
* Soil
* Road

The object

* reflects
* absorbs
* emits

different amounts of energy.

Each object has a unique **spectral signature**.

Example

Vegetation reflects a large amount of near-infrared light.

---

# Step 4: Recording of Energy by Sensor

Sensors measure reflected or emitted energy.

Sensors are mounted on

* Satellites
* Aircraft
* Drones

Examples

* Camera
* Scanner
* Radar

---

# Step 5: Transmission, Reception and Processing

Collected data are transmitted to a **ground station**.

Then computers perform:

* Error correction
* Noise removal
* Image enhancement
* Data storage

---

# Step 6: Interpretation and Analysis

The processed image is analyzed.

Interpretation can be:

### Visual

Humans examine images.

### Digital

Computers classify objects automatically.

---

# Step 7: Applications

The interpreted information is used in real-life problems.

---

# 6. Source of Energy

Energy comes from two sources.

## Natural Source

Sun

Examples:

* Landsat
* Sentinel-2

---

## Artificial Source

The sensor generates energy.

Examples

* Radar
* LiDAR

---

# 7. Interaction with Atmosphere

Atmospheric gases and particles modify electromagnetic waves.

Three important processes:

## Absorption

Energy is absorbed by gases like

* Water vapor
* Carbon dioxide
* Ozone

Result:

Less energy reaches the sensor.

---

## Scattering

Energy changes direction.

Types:

* Rayleigh scattering
* Mie scattering
* Non-selective scattering

Example:

Blue sky is due to Rayleigh scattering.

---

## Transmission

Energy passes through the atmosphere without much change.

Atmospheric windows allow energy to pass, making remote sensing possible.

---

# 8. Interaction with Target

When energy hits an object, three things occur.

```
Incident Energy

      ↓

Reflection

Absorption

Transmission
```

Energy balance:

$$
Incident = Reflected + Absorbed + Transmitted
$$

Different materials reflect different wavelengths.

Examples

Vegetation → high NIR reflection

Water → absorbs much infrared

Snow → highly reflective

---

# 9. Recording of Energy by Sensor

Sensors convert electromagnetic energy into digital signals.

Two main categories

## Passive Sensors

Measure natural energy.

Example

Satellite camera

---

## Active Sensors

Transmit their own energy and measure the return signal.

Examples

* Radar
* LiDAR

---

# 10. Transmission, Reception and Processing

The recorded signal is sent to Earth through communication systems.

Processing includes

* Calibration
* Noise removal
* Geometric correction
* Radiometric correction
* Enhancement

The output is a digital image.

---

# 11. Interpretation and Analysis

Purpose:

Convert image data into meaningful information.

Methods:

### Visual interpretation

Uses:

* Color
* Shape
* Size
* Texture
* Pattern
* Shadow

### Digital interpretation

Uses computer algorithms such as image classification.

Remote sensing has many applications.

* Agriculture
* Forestry
* Urban planning
* Disaster management
* Weather forecasting
* Mineral exploration
* Water resource management
* Military surveillance
* Environmental monitoring
* Oceanography

---

# 13. Advantages of Remote Sensing

* Covers large areas quickly
* No physical contact required
* Repeated observations over time
* Access to inaccessible areas
* Cost-effective for large regions
* Multi-spectral information available
* Useful for disaster monitoring
* Digital data are easy to store and analyze

---

# 14. Limitations of Remote Sensing

* Cloud cover affects optical images
* Expensive satellites and sensors
* Requires skilled interpretation
* Limited spatial resolution in some systems
* Atmospheric interference
* Cannot directly measure underground objects
* Large datasets require significant storage and processing

---

# 15. Ideal Remote Sensing System

An ideal remote sensing system should have:

* High spatial resolution
* High spectral resolution
* High temporal resolution (frequent revisits)
* High radiometric resolution
* Minimal atmospheric interference
* Accurate geometric positioning
* Fast data transmission
* Low operational cost
* Reliable sensors
* High accuracy and precision
