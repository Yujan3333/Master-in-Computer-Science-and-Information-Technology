## ✅ What is the Spiral Model?

The **Spiral Model** is a **risk-driven software development model** that combines features of the **Waterfall** and **Iterative** models.

It develops software in a **spiral of repeated cycles (iterations)**, where each cycle includes:

- Planning
    
- Risk analysis
    
- Development
    
- Evaluation
    

---

### 🔄 Phases in Each Spiral Cycle:

1. **Planning** – Define objectives, alternatives, and constraints
    
2. **Risk Analysis** – Identify and resolve risks (technical, financial, etc.)
    
3. **Engineering** – Design, code, and test the part of the system
    
4. **Evaluation** – Review with stakeholders and prepare for the next cycle
    

Each loop of the spiral results in a more complete version of the software.

---

### 🧠 Example:

You're building an e-commerce app.  
In the first spiral, you focus on user login.  
In the next, you add product listings.  
Before each phase, you analyze risks (e.g., payment issues, user load).

---

### ✅ Advantages:

- ✅ Good for **large and complex projects**
    
- ✅ Strong **risk management**
    
- ✅ **Flexible** to changes
    
- ✅ Continuous customer feedback
    

---

### ❌ Disadvantages:

- ❌ Can be **expensive** and **time-consuming**
    
- ❌ Needs **expert risk analysis**
    
- ❌ Not suitable for **small projects**
    

---

### 📌 Summary Line:

> The Spiral Model builds software in repeated cycles, focusing on **risk analysis** and **customer feedback** at each stage, making it ideal for large, complex projects with high uncertainty.
