# Contour Maps

## Definition

A **contour map** is a map that represents the **shape and elevation (height)** of the land using **contour lines**.

A **contour line** joins points having the **same elevation above mean sea level (MSL)**.

---

## Example

```text
        Hill

      300 m
      ______
    /        \
250 m        250 m
  /            \
200 m          200 m
 \              /
  \____________/
      150 m
```

Each contour line represents a **constant elevation**.

---

## Contour Interval

The **contour interval** is the **vertical difference in elevation between two consecutive contour lines**.

Example:

```text
100 m
110 m
120 m
130 m
```

Contour interval = **10 m**

---

# Characteristics of Contour Lines

1. Join points of **equal elevation**.
2. Never intersect each other (except in rare cases like overhanging cliffs).
3. Form closed loops.
4. Close contour lines indicate a **steep slope**.
5. Wide contour spacing indicates a **gentle slope**.

---

## Reading Contours

### Gentle Slope

```text
100

105

110

115
```

Lines are far apart → **Gentle slope**

---

### Steep Slope

```text
100
101
102
103
104
```

Lines are close together → **Steep slope**

---

### Hill

```text
      300
    250
  200
150
```

Elevation increases toward the center → **Hill**

---

### Depression

```text
150
200
250
300
```

Elevation decreases toward the center (often shown with hachure marks) → **Depression/Basin**

---

## Uses of Contour Maps

* Determining elevation
* Identifying hills and valleys
* Planning roads and railways
* Site selection for dams and buildings
* Watershed and drainage analysis
* Engineering and surveying

---

## Advantages

* Shows terrain shape clearly.
* Helps calculate slope and elevation.
* Useful for planning and construction.
* Widely used in GIS and topographic mapping.

---

## Disadvantages

* Difficult for beginners to interpret.
* Very steep or complex terrain may require many contour lines.
* Does not directly show land cover (e.g., vegetation or buildings).

---
