The **Waterfall Model** is a **linear and sequential software development model** in which each phase of the development process flows **downward like a waterfall**.

Each phase must be **completed fully before the next begins**, and there is **no overlapping** between phases.

---

### 📋 Phases of Waterfall Model:

1. **Requirement Analysis**
    
2. **System Design**
    
3. **Implementation (Coding)**
    
4. **Testing**
    
5. **Deployment**
    
6. **Maintenance**

### ✅ Advantages of the Waterfall Model

1. ✅ **Simple and easy to understand**  
    – Clear structure, easy for beginners and teams to follow
    
2. ✅ **Well-defined stages**  
    – Each phase has specific deliverables and a review process
    
3. ✅ **Easy to manage**  
    – Progress is easy to measure, especially with strict deadlines
    
4. ✅ **Good for small or well-understood projects**  
    – Works well when requirements are clear and fixed
    
5. ✅ **Document-driven**  
    – Helps with proper documentation and traceability
    

---

### ❌ Disadvantages of the Waterfall Model

1. ❌ **Inflexible to changes**  
    – Difficult to go back to a previous phase if requirements change
    
2. ❌ **Late testing**  
    – Errors or issues are found only after the full product is built
    
3. ❌ **Not ideal for complex or evolving projects**  
    – Poor choice for projects where requirements evolve during development
    
4. ❌ **High risk**  
    – A mistake in early stages can ruin the entire project
    
5. ❌ **No user feedback until the end**  
    – Customers see the product only after it's fully developed
