
## 🧠 **Complexity Theory**

### ✅ **Definition:**

**Complexity Theory** is a branch of **theoretical computer science** that studies the **amount of resources** (like **time**, **space**, or **processors**) needed to solve computational problems.

It helps us **classify problems** based on their **computational difficulty** and determines **how efficiently** problems can be solved using algorithms.

---

### 🔹 **Key Resources Measured:**

* **Time complexity**: Number of steps (instructions) an algorithm takes
* **Space complexity**: Amount of memory used during computation
* **Other resources**: Number of processors (in parallel computing), energy, communication steps

---

### 🔹 **Main Goals of Complexity Theory:**

1. 📊 **Classify problems into complexity classes** (e.g., P, NP, NP-complete, PSPACE)
2. ⏳ **Compare the efficiency of algorithms**
3. ❌ **Prove lower bounds** (how hard a problem is)
4. 🔁 **Understand the limits of computation**

---

### 🔹 **Example Classes:**

| Class           | Meaning                                 |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **P**           | Problems solvable in polynomial time    |
| **NP**          | Problems verifiable in polynomial time  |
| **NP-Complete** | Hardest problems in NP (e.g., SAT)      |
| **PSPACE**      | Problems solvable with polynomial space |

---

### 📝 **Exam-Style Answer:**

> **Complexity Theory** is the study of the **resources required** to solve computational problems using algorithms. It focuses on measuring **time and space** complexity and classifies problems into different **complexity classes** based on their difficulty.
>
> The theory helps identify whether a problem can be solved **efficiently** and what the **limits of computation** are. Major classes include **P**, **NP**, **NP-complete**, and **PSPACE**, which are used to group problems with similar resource requirements.
