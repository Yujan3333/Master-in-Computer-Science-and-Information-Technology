## 🐛 **The Morris Internet Worm (1988)**

---

### 📅 **What was it?**

The **Morris Worm** was the **first major computer worm** to spread across the **early internet (ARPANET)** in **November 1988**.

---

### 🧑‍💻 **Created by:**

- **Robert Tappan Morris**, a graduate student at **Cornell University**.
    
- Intended as a **harmless experiment** to measure the size of the internet — but it went out of control.
    

---

### ⚙️ **How it worked:**

The worm **exploited several vulnerabilities** in Unix systems:

1. **Buffer overflow** in the `finger` daemon
    
2. **Debug mode in `sendmail`**
    
3. **Trust relationships** using `.rhosts` and `rexec`
    
4. **Password guessing** (brute-force attacks on weak passwords)
    

---

### 📈 **Impact:**

- **Infected ~6,000 computers** (10% of the internet at the time)
    
- Caused massive **slowdowns, system crashes**, and disruptions
    
- Estimated **cost of damage**: **$10 million – $100 million**
    
- No data theft — just **denial of service** from system overload
    

---

### 🔐 **Security Lessons Learned:**

- Highlighted the **need for cybersecurity awareness**
    
- Led to the **formation of CERT (Computer Emergency Response Team)** at Carnegie Mellon University
    
- First case prosecuted under the **Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA)**
    

---

### ⚖️ **Legal Outcome:**

- Morris was the **first person convicted** under the CFAA (1986).
    
- Sentence:
    
    - **3 years probation**
        
    - **400 hours community service**
        
    - **$10,050 fine**
