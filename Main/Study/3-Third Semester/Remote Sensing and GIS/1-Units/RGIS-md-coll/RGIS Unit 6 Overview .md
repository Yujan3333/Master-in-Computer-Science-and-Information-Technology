# 1. Introduction

Remote sensing images alone cannot always provide accurate information. Therefore, field observations are needed to verify and improve the interpretation of satellite images.

This field verification process is called **Ground Truthing**.

To accurately record the location of field observations, **GNSS** (such as GPS) is used.

Thus, Unit 6 combines:

* **Ground Truth Data** (field data)
* **GNSS** (location data)

---

# 2. Ground Truth Data

## Definition

**Ground Truth Data** is the actual information collected directly from the Earth's surface to verify and validate remote sensing data.

In simple words,

> Ground truth means checking whether what the satellite image shows is actually true on the ground.

---

## Example

A satellite image classifies an area as a **forest**.

Researchers visit the location and observe:

* Tree species
* Tree density
* Tree height

If the field observation matches the image, the image interpretation is considered accurate.

---

## Why Ground Truth Data is Needed

* Verify satellite image interpretation.
* Improve classification accuracy.
* Correct image errors.
* Train image classification algorithms.
* Support scientific research.

---

# Applications

* Agriculture
* Forestry
* Land-use mapping
* Environmental monitoring
* Disaster assessment
* Urban planning

---

# 3. Requirements of Ground Truth Data

Good ground truth data should be:

### 1. Accurate

Measurements should correctly represent real conditions.

---

### 2. Representative

Samples should represent the entire study area.

---

### 3. Timely

Field data should be collected close to the date of satellite image acquisition.

---

### 4. Well Distributed

Samples should cover different land cover types.

---

### 5. Correctly Located

Coordinates should be accurately recorded using GNSS.

---

### 6. Consistent

The same methods should be used throughout the survey.

---

# 4. Instruments for Ground Truthing

Several instruments are used during field surveys.

---

## A. GNSS Receiver (GPS)

Used to determine precise geographic coordinates.

Applications:

* Record sampling locations
* Navigation
* Mapping

---

## B. Spectroradiometer

Measures the spectral reflectance of objects.

Applications:

* Crop analysis
* Soil analysis
* Vegetation studies

---

## C. Camera

Captures photographs of sampling sites.

Applications:

* Documentation
* Verification

---

## D. Measuring Tape

Measures dimensions of objects.

Applications:

* Tree height (with additional methods)
* Plot size
* Building dimensions

---

## E. Compass

Determines direction.

Applications:

* Navigation
* Orientation

---

## F. Field Notebook / Mobile Device

Used to record observations such as:

* Crop type
* Soil condition
* Weather
* Vegetation

# 6. Factors Affecting Spectral Measurement

Spectral measurements are influenced by many factors.

---

## A. Illumination

Amount and angle of sunlight affect reflectance.

More sunlight generally gives stronger reflected signals.

---

## B. Atmospheric Conditions

Clouds, haze, dust, and humidity modify the received radiation.

---

## C. Surface Moisture

Wet soil reflects differently from dry soil.

---

## D. Surface Roughness

Rough surfaces scatter energy more than smooth surfaces.

---

## E. Viewing Geometry

The angle between the sensor, object, and Sun affects measurements.

---

## F. Sensor Characteristics

Different sensors have different spectral sensitivity and resolution.

---

## G. Seasonal Variation

Vegetation changes with seasons, affecting spectral signatures.

---

# 7. Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS)

## Definition

**GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System)** is a satellite-based navigation system that provides **position, navigation, and timing (PNT)** information anywhere on Earth.

GNSS is used to determine:

* Latitude
* Longitude
* Altitude
* Time

# How GNSS Works

```text
GNSS Satellites
       │
Satellite Signals
       ▼
GNSS Receiver
       │
Calculates Position
       ▼
Latitude, Longitude, Altitude
```

A GNSS receiver measures the travel time of signals from **at least four satellites** to determine its position accurately.

---

# Components of GNSS

## 1. Space Segment

* Satellites orbiting the Earth.

---

## 2. Control Segment

* Ground control stations.
* Monitor satellite health.
* Update satellite information.

---

## 3. User Segment

* GNSS receivers used by people.

# Advantages of GNSS

* High positioning accuracy
* Worldwide coverage
* 24-hour operation
* Fast navigation
* Easy integration with GIS
* Supports remote sensing field surveys

---

# Limitations of GNSS

* Signal blockage by buildings, mountains, and dense forests
* Multipath errors (signals reflected from surfaces)
* Atmospheric effects (ionosphere and troposphere)
* Requires clear view of the sky
* Accuracy decreases in urban canyons

---

# GPS vs GNSS

| GPS                   | GNSS                                         |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| One navigation system | Collection of all global navigation systems  |
| Developed by USA      | Includes GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, BeiDou, etc. |
| Subset of GNSS        | Umbrella term                                |

**Remember:** **GPS is a type of GNSS, but GNSS is not only GPS.**
