🔄 Odd-Even Merge Sort vs 🦋 Bitonic Sort

🔍 Key Differences You've Discovered

Your handwritten example shows Odd-Even Merge Sort, not Bitonic Sort!

  • Odd-Even Merge Sort: Works with already sorted subsequences and merges them
  • Bitonic Sort: Works with bitonic sequences (monotonic up then down, or vice versa)
  • Both: Use butterfly networks but with different comparison patterns

📊 Summary: Why Your Example is Odd-Even Merge Sort

Evidence from your handwritten notes:

  • ✅ You start with two already sorted sequences
  • ✅ You separate into odd and even positions (I-phase)
  • ✅ You use "direct & cross" connections (II-phase)
  • ✅ Your network pattern matches odd-even merge, not bitonic distance-based comparisons

To convert the same data to Bitonic Sort:

  1. Reverse one sequence to create a bitonic input

  3. Each stage compares elements at fixed distances
