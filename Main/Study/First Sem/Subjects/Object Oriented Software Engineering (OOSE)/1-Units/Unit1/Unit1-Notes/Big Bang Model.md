### Big Bang Model Overview

The **Big Bang Model** is a straightforward and simple software development approach where:

- All resources are gathered, and the entire software system is developed **without much planning or analysis upfront**.
    
- The development is done in a single phase — coding, testing, and deployment happen at once.
    
- The final product is delivered only **after full completion** of the development phase.
    

---

### Key Features of Big Bang Model:

|Aspect|Description|
|---|---|
|**Planning**|Minimal or no formal planning or requirement analysis.|
|**Development**|All coding and development done at once.|
|**Testing**|Testing occurs only after the whole product is built.|
|**Delivery**|Delivered in a single release — no incremental builds.|
|**User Involvement**|Very low or none during development.|
|**Risk**|Very high risk because issues are found late.|
|**Best for**|Small, simple projects or experimental prototypes.|

---

### Advantages:

- Simple to understand and implement.
    
- Requires less overhead or documentation.
    
- Useful for small projects or proof-of-concept work.
    

---

### Disadvantages:

- High risk of failure due to lack of early testing and feedback.
    
- Difficult to estimate time and cost accurately.
    
- No early delivery of usable software.
    
- If requirements are unclear or change, it can cause major problems.
    

---

### When to Use Big Bang Model?

- When the project is small and simple.
    
- When requirements are well understood and unlikely to change.
    
- When quick prototyping or experimental development is needed.
